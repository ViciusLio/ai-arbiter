Arbiter at work

A day at Nordwind

Nordwind Consulting (invented) is an IT consulting firm. It decided one thing about AI: its people may use Claude, and nothing else. But they do not use Claude directly: they use tools, and some of those tools can switch to other engines with one click. This is what happens on an ordinary day, with Arbiter in between.

The rule of the firm

The rule, in one sentence

You may use the tools the firm declared, and only with the models it approved.

Approved: claude-sonnet-5-5 claude-haiku-4-5

Two more rules of the firm: a risky tool waits until a person has reviewed it, and a password or a token never leaves the firm.

The rule is a file, version 2026.10.1+consulting.2. Arbiter reads it and applies it to every request before the request leaves.

What happens

Giulia is chasing a bug in the bank's transfer code. To explain it she pastes a real case into Kiro: the e-mail address and the IBAN of one of the bank's customers. Kiro is set to Claude, the approved engine.

Stopped

Said plainly

  • The model and the tool server are stand-ins inside one process. No real provider and no real tool was called.
  • The product names are examples of how a firm labels its tools. Which engines a product offers, and whether it can be pointed at a gateway, is for each organisation to check with the vendor.
  • Arbiter sees what passes through it. A service opened in a browser does not: reading the logs of the company network is on the wish list.
  • Classifications are indicative, and the rule pack has not been reviewed by a person with legal training.
Every system and every request of a scenario is invented. Arbiter is a support tool. It does not provide legal advice.
